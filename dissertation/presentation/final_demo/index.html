Step 1 of 4, detection

writing-style reader

How this happens: two independent readers score the essay. One is a language model trained on matched pairs of real and AI-written essays to recognise machine word patterns. The other measures writing habits with formulas anyone can re-run. A small combiner turns the pair into one probability, and it only reads high when both agree.

Step 2 of 4, five measured habits

This essay's writing habits, against typical student writing

grey band = the middle 80% of real student essays  |  line = typical  |  dot = this essay, pushed toward AI, pushed toward human. For every habit the question is the same: does the dot sit inside the band of normal student writing?

How this happens: each habit is a simple measurement of the text, for example how varied the sentence lengths are, or how predictable each next word is. The same measurements were taken on hundreds of real student essays, so the grey band shows where students actually sit and the dot shows where this essay sits.

Where the detector's signal sits

How this happens: the system deletes one sentence at a time and watches the detector's score move, so the shaded sentences are the ones the detector itself reacted to. The phrase counts compare this essay against its matched twin, the same topic and length written by the other author.

Step 4 of 4, claims and questions

How this happens: a model trained on annotated argument essays finds the sentences where the student makes a claim, and keeps their sentence numbers so nothing is ever invented. The questions come from a second model, taught this exact task on this laptop (the technical term is fine-tuning). Every question is checked for form and labelled with the level of thinking it demands. The author of the essay can answer them from memory. Someone who pasted the essay cannot.
